# Findings

Generated by `python -m fraud.explain`. Every number is read from a file produced by a script in this repository. Statements marked as hypotheses are not tested here.

## 1. The strongest drivers are a mix of anonymised columns and card-level features

On 5,000 validation rows, the five features with the largest mean absolute SHAP value (log-odds) are C13 (0.654), card1_amt_mean (0.384), C5 (0.356), dist1 (0.317), C14 (0.303). Of the top 20, 8 are anonymised columns (C, D, M, V or id groups, named here only). The engineered and card-based groups overlap: 6 are features engineered in Phase 3 (card1_amt_mean, freq_card1, freq_card2, amt_cents, card1_amt_std, n_missing) and 6 are card columns or card-level aggregates (card1_amt_mean, freq_card1, card1, freq_card2, card2, card1_amt_std), with 4 features in both groups (card1_amt_mean, freq_card1, freq_card2, card1_amt_std). The remaining 4 are neither anonymised, engineered nor card-based (dist1, P_emaildomain, addr1, TransactionAmt).

Hypothesis: the weight on card-level aggregates and frequencies suggests the model partly recognises card-level patterns seen in the training window. This would help on cards that recur and may help less on new cards. It is not tested here.
Source: `reports/shap_top_features.csv`, `reports/figures/shap_bar.png`, `reports/figures/shap_beeswarm.png`.

## 2. Missed fraud concentrates in transactions without identity data and in one product code

At the chosen threshold on validation, ProductCD W has the highest false negative rate: 25.0% of its 1,485 frauds are missed (371), against 5.0% for ProductCD R (8 of 160). Transactions without identity data have a false negative rate of 24.9% (379 of 1,524 frauds) against 8.4% with identity data (128 of 1,518).

False alarms run the other way: the false positive rate is 18.2% with identity data and 8.1% without, and ProductCD C has the highest false positive rate at 23.3% (1,856 of 7,975 legitimate transactions).

Hypothesis: ProductCD W and the rows without identity data may overlap heavily, so these two gaps may be one gap seen twice. The overlap is not measured here.
Source: `reports/error_analysis.csv`, `reports/figures/error_analysis.png`.

## 3. PR-AUC is lower on test than on validation and does not decline steadily within the test window

Validation PR-AUC is 0.819 in the first bucket (days 119 to 126) and 0.603 in the last (days 140 to 151). Test buckets range from 0.534 to 0.636. Overall PR-AUC is 0.6853 on validation and 0.5879 on test, and the test 95% bootstrap interval (0.5673 to 0.6020, 200 resamples) does not include the validation value.

Test buckets do not decline steadily: in time order they score 0.632, 0.534, 0.541, 0.636, 0.603. The bucket fraud rate ranges from 3.1% to 4.2%. The overall fraud rate is 3.43% on validation and 3.48% on test, so a lower overall fraud rate does not account for the lower test PR-AUC.

Hypothesis: two effects may contribute. First, the fraud patterns may shift over time. Second, the validation score is optimistic because validation was used for early stopping, model choice and tuning. Because the test buckets show no steady decline, the data does not clearly favour drift over validation optimism, and the two are not separated here.
Source: `reports/stability.csv`, `reports/figures/stability.png`, `reports/metrics.json`, `reports/split_info.json`.

## 4. The cost-based threshold beats both reference policies on test

With a review fee of 5, the threshold chosen on validation costs 157,683.13 on test, against 469,608.11 for flagging nothing and 427,490.00 for flagging everything. It catches 78.1% of test frauds at a precision of 19.8%. These costs rest on illustrative assumptions.
Source: `reports/metrics.json` (`final_test`), `reports/cost_analysis.md`.

## Limitations

The threshold (2.82e-05) is on the raw model score scale. The scores are not calibrated probabilities: the SHAP base value is -13.53 in log-odds, so most scores sit very close to zero. At this threshold the model flags 12.3% of validation transactions (10,857 of 88,581) at a precision of 23.3%, and 13.7% of test transactions (12,165 of 88,581) at a precision of 19.8%. Most flagged transactions are therefore legitimate, which the review-fee assumption accepts but a real review team may not. The costs are illustrative. Validation was reused for early stopping, tuning and threshold choice, and there is a single chronological split. SHAP values come from a 5,000-row sample. They describe how the model uses each feature, not why fraud happens. Anonymised columns cannot be interpreted.
